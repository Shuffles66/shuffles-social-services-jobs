## 1. Purpose and commitment
We are building an **inclusive, accessible and respectful** marketplace that connects participants, workers and providers across the NDIS, aged-care and social-services sectors. Everyone is welcome here regardless of who they are. Discrimination, harassment and vilification have no place on our platform, and we take active steps to make sure people of all backgrounds and abilities can take part fully and safely.

## 2. Our values
- **Dignity** — every person is treated with respect and worth.
- **Choice and control** — participants and workers make their own decisions about who they work with.
- **Person-centred** — we put the individual at the centre of every interaction.
- **Strengths-based language** — we describe people by their strengths and goals, not deficits or labels.

## 3. Protected attributes
It is unlawful and against our rules to treat someone unfavourably because of a personal attribute, including (but not limited to): **disability** (and any associated aids, carers or assistance animals); **race, colour, national or ethnic origin** (including people from CALD backgrounds); **sex, gender identity and sexual orientation** (including LGBTQIA+ people); **age**; **religion or religious belief**; **marital, relationship, family or carer status**; **pregnancy or breastfeeding**; and **political opinion**. This protection applies whether the attribute is actual, assumed or held by an associate.

## 4. What is not allowed
The following are prohibited on the platform:
- **Discriminatory listings, profiles or messages** — e.g. job ads, needs or worker profiles that exclude people on protected grounds.
- **Harassment** — unwelcome conduct (including comments, images or messaging) that demeans, intimidates or offends.
- **Vilification** — inciting hatred, contempt or ridicule of a person or group based on a protected attribute.
- **Refusing service** on a protected ground, or victimising someone for raising a discrimination concern.

> **Lawful participant choices are not discrimination.** A participant exercising genuine choice and control — for example, choosing a worker of a **particular gender** for personal-care reasons, or a worker who speaks their **language** or shares their **cultural or religious background** — is making a legitimate, person-centred choice, not discriminating. These preferences are expressly permitted; they are about safety, dignity and culturally appropriate care, not exclusion.

## 5. Accessibility and CALD inclusion
The platform includes built-in accessibility and CALD features so more people can use it independently: **read-aloud (text-to-speech)**, **high-contrast mode**, **larger text**, **multilingual content/translation**, and **voice input**. We aim to publish key information in **plain English** and **Easy Read** where practical. For people who need language support, **interpreters** are available through **TIS National on 131 450**, and people who are deaf or have a hearing or speech impairment can connect via the **National Relay Service (NRS) on 133 677**.

## 6. Reasonable adjustments
We make **reasonable adjustments** so people with disability or other needs can take part on an equal basis — for example, alternative formats, extra time, a support person or advocate, or a different contact method. Tell us what you need at {{support URL / email}} and we will work with you to find a workable solution, unless doing so would cause unjustifiable hardship.

## 7. Inclusive language and imagery
Listings, profiles and shared assets should use **respectful, inclusive, strengths-based language** and **representative imagery**. Use person-first or identity-first language as the person prefers, avoid stereotypes, and describe roles by the work and the support required rather than assumptions about who can do it. See our best-practice asset guidance (`docs/BEST-PRACTICE-ASSETS.md`) for examples and templates.

## 8. Reporting discrimination
If you experience or witness discrimination, harassment or vilification, please tell us:
- **Online help desk / Support:** {{support URL}} · **Email:** {{complaints@…}} · **Phone:** {{phone}}
- You can report **through an advocate or support person**, or **anonymously**, and accessible options (interpreter, Easy Read, NRS) apply — see our Complaints Policy.

You can also contact an external body at any time — you do not have to come to us first:
- **Australian Human Rights Commission** — **1300 656 419** · **humanrights.gov.au**
- The relevant **state or territory equal-opportunity / anti-discrimination commission**, and (for in-scope matters) the **NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission on 1800 035 544**.

## 9. Consequences of breaches
Breaches are taken seriously and handled fairly under our Complaints process. Depending on severity, action may include: a warning, removal or editing of the offending listing/message, mandatory acknowledgement of this policy, **suspension or permanent removal** of an account, and referral to the police or a regulator where the conduct may be unlawful. No person will be disadvantaged for raising a concern in good faith.

## 10. Review
We review this policy at least **annually** and after any significant incident, complaint trend or change in the law, and we update our accessibility, CALD and language-support features as the platform grows.

### Plain-English summary (for participants)
*Everyone is welcome here — no matter your disability, background, culture, language, religion, gender, who you love, or your age. Being treated unfairly for who you are is not allowed. You can still choose a worker who suits you — for example, the same gender or who speaks your language — that is your choice, not discrimination. We have read-aloud, bigger text, high-contrast and translation tools, and you can use an interpreter on **131 450** or the relay service on **133 677**. If someone treats you unfairly, tell us — or call the Human Rights Commission on **1300 656 419**.*

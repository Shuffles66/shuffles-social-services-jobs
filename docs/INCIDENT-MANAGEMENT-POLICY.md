## 1. Purpose
This policy explains how we identify, respond to, record, manage and learn from incidents — and how we meet our obligations to notify **reportable incidents** to the NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission. Our first priority is always the safety, health and wellbeing of people with disability. We treat every incident seriously, act quickly, and use what we learn to prevent harm from happening again.

## 2. What is an incident vs a reportable incident
An **incident** is any act, omission, event or circumstance that has caused, or could have caused, harm to a person with disability — including near misses. All incidents are recorded and managed under this policy.

A **reportable incident** is a more serious category that **must be notified to the NDIS Commission** (whether it happened during, or in connection with, the supports or services provided). Reportable incidents are:
- the **death** of a person with disability;
- **serious injury** of a person with disability;
- **abuse or neglect** of a person with disability;
- **unlawful sexual or physical contact with, or assault of,** a person with disability;
- **sexual misconduct** committed against, or in the presence of, a person with disability, including grooming; and
- the **use of a restrictive practice** in relation to a person with disability, **other than where the use is in accordance with an authorisation** (and any behaviour support plan) — i.e. an **unauthorised restrictive practice**.

If you are unsure whether something is reportable, **treat it as if it is** and tell the {{Incident / Safeguarding Officer}} immediately.

## 3. Immediate response
The first response to any incident is to **make people safe**:
1. **Ensure safety first** — attend to the immediate safety, health and wellbeing of the person and anyone else affected.
2. **Call 000** if anyone is in danger, seriously injured, or a crime may have occurred (police, ambulance, fire).
3. **Support the person** — provide reassurance and any support they need, in a way that respects their dignity, choices and communication needs.
4. **Preserve evidence** — do not disturb the scene any more than is needed for safety; keep relevant items, messages and records intact.
5. **Notify** the {{Incident / Safeguarding Officer}} as soon as possible so reporting timeframes can be met.

## 4. Recording
Every incident — reportable or not, including near misses — is logged in our **incident register ({{system — e.g. the platform's incident log / spreadsheet / ticketing tool}})**. Each record must capture, at a minimum:
- the **date, time and location** of the incident;
- the **people involved** (the person with disability, workers, witnesses) and their roles;
- a clear, factual **description** of what happened;
- the **immediate actions taken** (including any first aid, 000 call, or safety steps); and
- whether the incident is assessed as **reportable**, and the date/time of any notification to the NDIS Commission.
Records are made promptly and kept securely (see §8).

## 5. Reporting timeframes to the NDIS Commission
Reportable incidents are notified to the NDIS Commission **as required by the Rules — confirm the current timeframes on the NDIS Commission website before relying on this**. As a general guide:
- **Most reportable incidents** — notify **within 24 hours** of becoming aware.
- A **death**, **serious injury**, **abuse or neglect**, **unlawful sexual or physical contact/assault**, or **sexual misconduct** — notify **within 24 hours**.
- The **unauthorised use of a restrictive practice** — notify within the timeframe set by the Rules ({{commonly within 5 business days; confirm}}).
- A **final / more detailed report** (the further information the Commission requires) is usually provided **within 5 business days** of the initial notification.

Notifications are submitted through the NDIS Commission's **reportable incidents portal** by the {{Incident / Safeguarding Officer}} (or {{delegate}}). ## 6. Roles and responsibilities
- **All workers, contractors, providers and representatives** — act to keep people safe, respond immediately, and report every incident to the {{Incident / Safeguarding Officer}} straight away; never delay reporting.
- **Incident / Safeguarding Officer ({{name / role}})** — assesses whether an incident is reportable, ensures notifications meet the required timeframes, manages the incident register, oversees fair and safe handling, and supports those involved.
- **Management** — ensures resources and training, oversees compliance, reviews trends, and drives improvement and any Commission notifications and follow-up.

## 7. Supporting people involved
People involved in an incident are treated with care and respect:
- **No detriment** — no person will be disadvantaged, lose services, or be treated unfairly for reporting an incident, supporting a report, or being involved in one in good faith.
- **Support and advocacy** — the person with disability (and their family, carers or nominees) can have an **advocate** or support person involved at any stage, and we will provide or refer to **counselling** and other support as needed.
- **Accessible communication** — we communicate in the person's preferred format and language, using interpreters, Easy Read or the National Relay Service where needed.

## 8. Privacy and confidentiality
We collect and use only the information needed to manage the incident and meet our obligations, in line with the **Privacy Act 1988 (Cth)** and the Australian Privacy Principles. Incident records are stored securely, access is limited to those who need it to manage the matter, and information is shared only where the law or the NDIS Commission requires, or with consent. Personal information about the person with disability is handled with particular care and never published.

## 9. Review and continuous improvement
After each incident we review what happened and what can be improved, and we **analyse trends** across the incident register at least {{quarterly}} to identify patterns, root causes and systemic issues. We use this to **prevent recurrence** — updating processes, training, supports and this policy. This policy is reviewed at least **annually** and after any significant incident or regulatory change.

## 10. How this links to Complaints and Safeguarding
This policy works together with our other safeguards:
- **Complaints** — a complaint may reveal an incident, and an incident may lead to a complaint; serious complaints are assessed against this policy and escalated where they are reportable. See the **Complaints Management and Resolution Policy**.
- **Safeguarding** — incidents inform our broader safeguarding, risk and worker-screening practices.
- **NDIS Quality and Safeguards Commission** — anyone can contact the Commission at any time:
  - **Phone:** 1800 035 544 (free from landlines) · **TTY:** 133 677
  - **Online:** ndiscommission.gov.au
  - **Interpreters** available on request.

### Plain-English summary (for participants)
*If something goes wrong or someone is hurt, we put safety first — we help the person, call 000 if anyone is in danger, and write down what happened. Serious things (like injury, abuse, neglect or assault) are reported to the NDIS Commission quickly, usually within 24 hours. You can have an advocate or support person, you can get counselling, and you will never be treated badly for speaking up. You can call the NDIS Commission any time on **1800 035 544**.*
